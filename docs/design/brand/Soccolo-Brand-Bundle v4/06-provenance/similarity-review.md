# Soccolo, similarity review

Review date: **Sep 14, 2026**. Reconfirmed at v1.1, when the letterforms changed to a licensed typeface; the symbol, which is what this review turns on, was not changed in substance. Reviewer: Claude (`claude-opus-5`), at the
direction of Syed Ahmed.

**Founder decision recorded 14 September 2026: APPROVED FOR USE WITH DISCLOSED
RISK; NOT INDEPENDENTLY CLEARED OR REGISTERED.** The exact-name knockout
evidence is encouraging, but the standalone/combined device has an acknowledged
material double-C risk and has not received a register,
figurative/device-code, class-specific or qualified professional assessment.
Syed Ahmed expressly declined to make an IP Australia or external clearance
process a condition of SP-076 completion. That is a business risk-acceptance
decision, not a clearance conclusion or non-infringement warranty.

## 0. What this is, and what it is not

**This is not a trade mark clearance search and must not be relied on as one.**

It is a recorded design-stage similarity check: what was searched, on what date,
what was found, and which conflicts remain unresolved. Its purpose is to
document that the question was asked before the design was fixed, and to hand a
clearance attorney a head start.

### Scope actually searched

| Source | Query | Date |

| Public web search | `"Soccolo" football soccer academy app` | Sep 14, 2026 |
| Public web search | Interlocking double-C logo trade mark protection | Sep 14, 2026 |

### Scope NOT searched, and it is the part that counts

- **No trade mark register was searched.** Not IP Australia, not EUIPO, not
  USPTO, not the WIPO Global Brand Database, not any national register.
- **No image or device-code similarity search** was run. Figurative marks are
  indexed by Vienna classification codes; that search was not performed and is
  the search that finds a conflicting device mark.
- **No company, domain or app-store name availability check** beyond the general
  web searches above.
- **No class-specific search.** The relevant classes for a football training
  product are likely to include Nice classes 9, 41 and 42, and possibly 25 and
  28 if merchandise or equipment follow. None were searched.

An attorney-run clearance search across those sources remains **outstanding and
recommended**, particularly before filing or substantial spend on physical
application of the mark. The founder has accepted proceeding without making it
a repository-activity completion gate.

## 1. Findings on the name

No brand, company, product or application trading as **"Soccolo"** was found in
the public web search performed. The nearest results were unrelated businesses
with partially similar names (`Socolive`, `SoCoo'c`, `SOCO`, `Socimi`), none in
sports training, none using a comparable device.

That is a weakly encouraging signal and nothing more. A name can be registered,
in use, or pending without appearing in a general web search, which is precisely
why the register searches above are outstanding. Note also that the name sits
close to the generic root "soccer", which cuts both ways: it aids recall, and it
may attract objections or weaken the scope of protection on distinctiveness
grounds. That is a question for the clearance attorney.

## 2. Finding on the symbol, and it is the material one

### The conflict

**Two opposed letter C forms is the configuration of the Chanel monogram, and
Chanel enforces it vigorously and successfully, including against figurative
marks that are not identical and that sit outside its core classes.**

Reported outcomes located in the search include Chanel obtaining cancellation of
a registration for an interlocking figurative design, and succeeding against an
imitation of its interlocking C monogram; alongside at least one jurisdiction
(Japan) where Chanel did not succeed in blocking a registration. The pattern is
consistent with a mark that is treated as well known, defended broadly, and
litigated often.

The sketch that preceded this work placed two opposed C forms in close
proximity, with the standalone symbol showing them **overlapping**. That
overlapping, opposed configuration is the closest point of approach to the
Chanel monogram and was treated as the primary risk in this design.

### What was changed in response

Three features of the rebuilt symbol are there specifically to create distance.
They are structural, not cosmetic, and they should not be edited away:

1. **The two forms never touch or overlap.** A measured gap of 13.61 units
   (0.72 of the stroke) separates the two forms, and a further 8.51 units
   (0.45 of the stroke) separates each form from the central dot. An
   interlocking monogram is defined by its interlock; this mark cannot
   interlock without being rebuilt.
2. **The dot is load-bearing.** It is not an ornament between two letters. Remove
   it and the composition is incomplete. The Chanel monogram has no
   corresponding element.
3. **The terminals are cut square across the stroke at a fixed ±27.3° aperture**,
   giving four blunt ends. The Chanel monogram's forms are tapered and calligraphic
   in character. The letterform language is different at the level of the
   stroke.

Further distancing factors, which are relevant but weaker because they can
change: a different colourway (mint or deep green with navy, rather than black),
a different field of use (sports training software, not fashion, leather goods
or cosmetics), and use that will in practice be dominated by the full wordmark
rather than the bare symbol.

### Residual risk, stated plainly

**The risk is reduced, not eliminated.** Two opposed C forms remain two opposed
C forms. A well-known mark can be asserted across classes on dilution or
reputation grounds in many jurisdictions, and the fact that a design was
independently constructed is not itself a defence to a trade mark claim, however
well documented. The construction record in
`rights-and-provenance.md` helps against a copying allegation; it does not
answer a likelihood-of-confusion or dilution argument.

### The de-risked route, already built

The **Orbit** alternate (`02-logo/symbol/soccolo-symbol-orbit-*.svg`) is a
different idea, not a variation: two broken concentric rings turning around the
same dot, and like the primary it is constructed rather than taken from the
typeface. It contains no letter C, no opposed pair and no monogram reading. It
is square, which solves the app-tile and 16 px favicon problems the primary mark
has.

It is supplied finished, to the same standard, so that **if clearance advice
comes back unfavourable on the double C, the identity does not have to be
restarted.** The wordmark, palette, lockup system, spacing rules, app assets and
export treatment are all unaffected by swapping the symbol.

That was the reason for building it. Treat it as insurance that has already been
paid for.

## 3. Findings on other elements

| Element | Assessment |
|---|---|
| Wordmark letterforms | Set in Poppins SemiBold under the SIL Open Font License, which expressly permits use in a logo and registration of the result as a trade mark. Delivered as outlines, so no font data ships. The letterforms are therefore not exclusive: anyone with the same free font can set the same word. That is a limit on artwork exclusivity, not on trade mark protection for the name. |
| Tagline "Train, Play, Grow" | Set in the same face. Short laudatory phrases of this kind are widely used and generally weakly distinctive. Likely to be difficult to protect and correspondingly low-risk to adopt, but **not searched**. Flag it to the attorney: three-word taglines in the sports and fitness space collide often. |
| Palette | Colour values are not protectable as such. Single-colour trade marks exist but are rare and narrow. No concern. |
| Orbit alternate | Concentric broken rings are a common visual idiom (targets, loading indicators, wireless symbols). That commonality cuts both ways: low distinctiveness, therefore lower conflict risk but also weaker protection. **Not searched.** |

## 5. Recommendation

1. Consider an attorney clearance search covering conflicts 1 to 4 before a
   trade-mark filing or major physical-production commitment.
2. Give the attorney this document, `rights-and-provenance.md`, and the
   generator source. The construction record is useful evidence of independent
   creation.
3. Ask specifically about the double-C configuration. Do not let it pass as a
   detail. It is the one finding here that could require a redesign.
4. Hold the Orbit alternate in reserve until that advice is in.
5. If professional clearance is later commissioned, perform it before
   irreversible merchandise, signage and embroidery commitments.
