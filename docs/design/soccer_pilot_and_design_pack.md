**1. Age-dependent experience**

The following are proposed product segments, not official coaching categories. Younger-player guidance is informed by the emphasis on fun, basic ball control, and repetition in Football Australia and FIFA resources. The specific scripts, age restrictions, and timing below require coach review. [MiniRoos](https://playfootball.com.au/miniroos), [FIFA ball-control guidance for ages 4–8](https://www.fifatrainingcentre.com/en/practice/grassroots/grassroots-and-youth-football-essentials/grassroots-coaching-essentials/coaching-children-to-master-ball-control.php).

| Age band | Interface and content style | Proposed assistance | Progress emphasis |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5–7 | One instruction at a time, large picture buttons, spoken prompts, simple play missions | Adult stays nearby and handles setup, recording, and review | Participation, confidence, basic control |
| 8–10 | Short skill missions, simple choices, optional position exploration | Adult sets up and remains available | Repeatable basic movements and personal milestones |
| 11–13 | More combinations, selectable difficulty, scanning and role explanations | Parent setup initially; partner where specified | Technique criteria and skill combinations |
| 14–17 | Mature sports layout, position programs, more demanding decision variants | Parent controls account; independent practice where suitable | Personal goals, repeatable assessment conditions |
| 18 | Athlete layout with own account, storage, purchases, and consent | Self-directed; partner where specified | Personal practice and assessment history |

Age does not determine technical level. An 18-year-old beginner should see beginner drills without childlike language. A skilled younger player can receive an appropriate progression without bypassing age suitability or supervision requirements. “Expert challenge” describes a reviewed exercise variant, not a talent score.

Reconfirm age through the chosen age/ownership process. Offer adult account control when a player reaches 18; do not silently preserve parental viewing rights merely because the family continues paying. The exact transition and verification process needs privacy review before implementation.

**2. Pilot drill catalog**

All drills are **DRAFT — COACH REVIEW REQUIRED**, version 0.1, English. No reviewer has signed off. All dimensions, sets, repetitions, and work/rest times are production and usability starting points, not individualized training prescriptions. The coach must approve them, adapt them to the age/ability combination, and define safe substitutions before anyone is asked to practise them in an app trial.

For each drill, supply original demonstration assets, a setup image, short spoken cues, captions, a useful alternate view, and a slow version. Use an adult movement reference with appropriate rights. **D01 — Inside touches**

- **Goal and prerequisites:** move the ball gently between the inside surfaces of the feet; able to stand and move comfortably around a stationary ball.
- **Setup:** one ball and a clear, level area; begin with an approximately 2 × 2 m demonstration footprint. Camera must show both feet, ball, and full body.
- **Instruction:** start with the ball between the feet; make a soft inside touch to the other foot; repeat at a controllable pace; stop the ball when cued.
- **Cue script:** “Soft touches. Small steps. Keep it close.” Use a single cue at a time for ages 5–7.
- **Draft timing:** 2 × 20 seconds with 30 seconds to rest/reset; coach may replace timed work with a short play task for the youngest group.
- **Common mistakes and review:** touches travel beyond reach or the player rushes. Proposed review criterion: controlled alternating touches without repeatedly chasing the ball; count only when a parent/coach actually observes.
- **Variants:** easier: stop between touches; harder: move slowly along a lane; expert concept: respond to a direction cue while maintaining control, subject to review.
- **Assets and camera:** front-quarter main view and a foot-level close-up; front camera for practice. Animation brief A01 below.

**D02 — Sole rolls**

- **Goal and prerequisites:** move and stop the ball using the sole; comfortable balance beside the ball.
- **Setup:** one ball, level clear area, approximately 2 × 2 m demonstration footprint; stable surface and footwear selected by the coach.
- **Instruction:** gently place the sole on the ball, roll it across the body, receive or stop it, then repeat to the other side.
- **Cue script:** “Gently roll. Stop. Switch sides.”
- **Draft timing:** 2 × 20 seconds, 30 seconds rest/reset.
- **Common mistakes and review:** standing heavily on the ball, reaching beyond balance, or uncontrolled rolls. Review controlled movement and stopping rather than speed.
- **Variants:** easier: single roll with a complete reset; harder: link a roll to an inside touch; advanced variant: roll and exit in a called direction.
- **Assets and camera:** front-quarter and side views showing the supporting leg and ball contact; front camera with room for lateral movement.

**D03 — Dribble and stop**

- **Goal and prerequisites:** carry the ball within reach and stop under control; D01 or equivalent basic control.
- **Setup:** one ball, two flat markers, approximately 4 m clear lane for the reference layout; coach scales distance and usable run-off area.
- **Instruction:** travel slowly with short touches, stop at the marker or spoken cue, settle the ball, and restart in the other direction.
- **Cue script:** “Little touches. Stop the ball. Look around.”
- **Draft timing:** 4 short journeys with a reset after each; an adult controls pace for the youngest group.
- **Common mistakes and review:** touching the ball too far ahead or stopping outside the planned area. Review whether the player can stop deliberately while remaining balanced.
- **Variants:** easier: walk and stop on a fixed mark; harder: use a spoken stop cue; advanced: choose a turn direction after stopping.
- **Assets and camera:** wide diagonal view containing the full lane and run-off; avoid a camera position in the ball’s path.

**D04 — Cone weave**

- **Goal and prerequisites:** change direction with controlled touches; D03 or equivalent.
- **Setup:** one ball and four cones; approximately 4 × 3 m reference layout with a clear return route. Spacing is a coach-controlled variable.
- **Instruction:** approach the first cone slowly, guide the ball around each marker, finish under control, then walk back to reset.
- **Cue script:** “Small touches. Around the cone. Finish in control.”
- **Draft timing:** 4 passes through the course with a reset between passes.
- **Common mistakes and review:** cutting across the course, wide uncontrolled touches, or chasing a time score. Review the intended route and control at direction changes.
- **Variants:** easier: wider spacing and two cones; harder: alternate feet or use a narrower reviewed layout; expert concept: a late exit-direction cue.
- **Assets and camera:** wide three-quarter view plus overhead setup view; rear camera with helper or a front camera proven to cover the full route. Animation brief A03 below.

**D05 — Pass through a gate**

- **Goal and prerequisites:** direct a ground pass toward a target; comfortable approach to a stationary ball.
- **Setup:** one ball and two cones forming a gate. Reference starts at a modest distance, approximately 2–3 m, with a wide gate and safe ball retrieval space.
- **Instruction:** settle the ball, look at the target, make a controlled inside-foot pass, then retrieve or receive the ball for the next attempt.
- **Cue script:** “Look at the gate. Pass gently. Reset.”
- **Draft timing:** 6 attempts; add the other foot only when appropriate.
- **Common mistakes and review:** excessive power, unsafe retrieval, or starting before the area is clear. Review passes through the gate under the same setup conditions.
- **Variants:** easier: shorter distance/wider gate; harder: vary the target or receive before passing; advanced: choose between two gates.
- **Assets and camera:** diagonal side view shows approach, contact, and target; position the phone outside the passing line.

**D06 — Receive and return**

- **Goal and prerequisites:** control a ground pass and return it; D05 or equivalent.
- **Setup:** one ball, partner or suitable rebound surface, and markers for receiving positions; begin with a short comfortable distance approved by the coach.
- **Instruction:** prepare to receive, take a controlled first touch, return the ball toward the partner or marked rebound target, and reset.
- **Cue script:** “Ready. Cushion the ball. Pass back.”
- **Draft timing:** 6 receptions, reset after each; repeat on the other side if approved.
- **Common mistakes and review:** first touch runs away, return is rushed, or partner service is inconsistent. Review touch control and return direction separately.
- **Variants:** easier: slow predictable service and a pause; harder: first touch to a marked side; advanced: vary service side or target with a cue.
- **Assets and camera:** receiver-side three-quarter view with the ball’s arrival and departure visible. Animation brief A02 below.

**D07 — Open-body receive**

- **Goal and prerequisites:** receive with a body orientation that permits the next pass; D06 control established.
- **Setup:** partner, one ball, receiving marker, and a second passing target; clear safe routes for both ball and player.
- **Instruction:** observe the intended next target, prepare the body position, receive into usable space, then pass toward that target.
- **Cue script:** “See your next pass. Receive into space. Play.”
- **Draft timing:** 6 controlled receive-and-pass repetitions with resets.
- **Common mistakes and review:** predetermined blind turns or a touch across an unsafe line. Review orientation and whether the first touch supports the chosen pass.
- **Variants:** easier: fixed target; harder: two possible targets; expert concept: late partner cue with consistent, reviewed service.
- **Assets and camera:** wide diagonal scene showing server, receiver, and target; camera assisted where necessary. Do not claim a single fixed body angle is correct for every situation.

**D08 — Pull-back turn**

- **Goal and prerequisites:** change direction while retaining possession; D02 and D03 or equivalents.
- **Setup:** ball, approach lane, turn marker, and clear exit lane.
- **Instruction:** approach under control, settle into the turn, draw the ball back with the sole, turn into the open lane, and move away with controlled touches.
- **Cue script:** “Slow down. Pull back. Turn into space.”
- **Draft timing:** 4 turns with reset, then review suitability of changing foot or direction.
- **Common mistakes and review:** unstable weight over the ball or an abrupt turn into an obstacle. Review control before and after the turn.
- **Variants:** easier: stationary pull-back and turn; harder: gentle moving approach; advanced: select the exit on a cue.
- **Assets and camera:** side-quarter view preserves visibility of sole contact, supporting foot, and exit.

**D09 — Feint and escape**

- **Goal and prerequisites:** use a simple deceptive movement followed by a controlled change of direction; D04 control established.
- **Setup:** ball, one marker representing an obstacle, and two open exit gates; no live defender in the initial drill.
- **Instruction:** approach slowly, make the coach-selected simple body feint, take the ball into an open exit, and finish under control.
- **Cue script:** “Show one way. Take the ball away.”
- **Draft timing:** 4 repetitions each side if approved, with resets.
- **Common mistakes and review:** copying exaggerated animation, tangling feet, or accelerating beyond space. Review a coherent movement and controlled exit.
- **Variants:** easier: walk through the movement; harder: choose either exit; expert concept: respond to a partner cue, not contact pressure.
- **Assets and camera:** wide front-quarter scene showing marker, both feet, and exit; keep the recording phone outside either exit route.

**D10 — Receive and finish to a target**

- **Goal and prerequisites:** combine a controlled reception with a directed finish; D05 and D06 or equivalents.
- **Setup:** ball, partner serving an appropriate ground pass, safe goal or marked target, and clear retrieval space; target type and power set by the coach.
- **Instruction:** receive into a useful space, look at the target, make a controlled finish, then wait for the next service.
- **Cue script:** “First touch. Find the target. Finish in control.”
- **Draft timing:** 6 attempts with resets.
- **Common mistakes and review:** prioritizing force, striking before the area is clear, or chasing an uncontrolled reception. Review reception and target accuracy separately.
- **Variants:** easier: stationary pass into a wide target; harder: receive from a different side; advanced: choose between two target zones.
- **Assets and camera:** side view includes server, player, and target; adult operates the phone outside the ball path. No bystanders behind the target.

**D11 — Scan, choose, and pass**

- **Goal and prerequisites:** gather information and select a pass; D06 and D07 established.
- **Setup:** partner, ball, and two clearly labelled target gates. Use different shapes or numbers as well as colors so the choice is not color-dependent.
- **Instruction:** identify the partner’s cue before receiving, control the incoming ball, and pass to the matching target.
- **Cue script:** “Check the cue. Receive. Find the gate.”
- **Draft timing:** 6 decisions with resets; begin with early, predictable cues.
- **Common mistakes and review:** guessing without looking or increasing speed before control. Review correct choice and pass execution as separate observed outcomes.
- **Variants:** easier: static ball and an early cue; harder: cue before reception; expert concept: reviewed combinations with additional targets and consistent service.
- **Assets and camera:** show cue, receiver, and both gates. Use partner assistance; the child should not read a phone while dribbling.

**D12 — Goalkeeper set and ground-ball collection**

- **Goal and prerequisites:** establish a ready position and collect an appropriate rolling ball; comfortable basic movement, adult/coach assistance, and suitable surface.
- **Setup:** partner, suitable ball, short rolling lane, and level clear area. Ground balls only; no diving, collisions, or overhead service.
- **Instruction:** move to the ready position, watch the partner’s controlled roll, collect using the coach-approved technique, return the ball, and reset.
- **Cue script:** “Ready. Watch the ball. Gather and reset.”
- **Draft timing:** 6 gentle services with resets.
- **Common mistakes and review:** difficult or fast service, unsuitable technique, or collecting too close to obstacles. A goalkeeper-qualified reviewer must define the actual collection method and success criteria.
- **Variants:** easier: stationary ball and setup practice; harder: small positioning movement before a predictable roll. More demanding goalkeeper work is a separate reviewed asset.
- **Assets and camera:** front-quarter view outside the rolling line; capture full body, hands, and ball. Include both service and collection context.

**Pilot program assembly**

Use three short session templates that refer to the approved age/ability variants: **Ball Control** (D01, D02, D03), **Passing Foundations** (D05, D06, D07 where suitable), and **Move and Choose** (D04, D09, D11 where suitable). D08, D10, and D12 support role exploration and supplementary sessions. When a drill is unsuitable for a player, substitute an approved exercise; never fill a session by raising the age or difficulty gate automatically.

The coach determines warm-up, rest, total duration, and progression. A 12-drill catalog is a pilot set, not comprehensive coverage of every position or ability level.

**3. Animation production briefs**

Shared art direction: original, human-proportioned football avatar; plain kit; visible boots and ball; simple bright training environment; clearly readable foot contact; restrained scenery. The reference demonstration should be performed by an appropriately skilled adult. Any youth-proportioned avatar must preserve plausible movement and receive a fresh visual review after retargeting.

Keep the ball as a separately controlled asset. Body motion capture does not establish a correct ball path or contact event. An animator must check foot sliding, ground penetration, ball deformation, implausible acceleration, and hidden contacts. A coach signs off on the rendered result, not just the written exercise.

Deliver editable project sources, linked assets with commercial rights, a main demonstration, alternate view, slow demonstration, setup still, poster still, captions, and separate narration. Proposed delivery: 720p MP4 for in-app use and a higher-resolution master for later reuse; final codec, bitrate, and asset budget follow device and download testing. Keep instructional text out of the rendered movement where possible so it can be localized independently.

**A01 — Close ball control: Inside touches / D01**

| Element | Production instruction |
|---|---|
| Learning outcome | Clearly show the ball passing between the inside of the feet with controllable touches |
| Environment | Level bright surface; simple boundary markers; one ball; no opponent |
| Main view | Front three-quarter, full body and ball visible; feet remain large enough to understand contact |
| Alternate view | Foot-level close-up for contact plus a side view if it resolves a coaching question |
| Setup shot | Approximately 3 seconds: avatar and ball stationary; indicate the usable practice space |
| Teaching shot | Approximately 8 seconds: slow, distinct touches with a brief reset so contact can be inspected |
| Practice loop | Approximately 8 seconds: repeat a small coherent sequence; choose matching start/end pose and ball state |
| Closing shot | Approximately 3 seconds: control the ball to a stop and return to ready position |
| Narration draft | “Start with the ball between your feet. Use soft inside touches. Keep it close.” |
| Younger-player version | One cue per clip; longer visible reset; no speed challenge |
| Older-player version | Less introductory narration; optional movement variant only after separate approval |
| Acceptance | Correct contact surface; plausible balance; stable ground contact; ball remains in view; coach can explain each cue from the visual |

Render the main view and slow demonstration as separate controlled exports. Do not create a slow visual by slowing the spoken instruction. The 22-second teaching sequence and 8-second loop are proposed edit lengths, not exercise duration prescriptions.

| Element | Production instruction |

| Environment | Main player, neutral partner or explicit rebound surface, one ball, receiving markers |
| Main view | Diagonal receiver-side view that preserves arrival path, first touch, and return direction |
| Alternate view | Wider setup view and a close view of first-touch contact |
| Setup shot | Approximately 4 seconds: show both positions and the safe ball route |
| Receive shot | Approximately 6 seconds: predictable ground pass and clearly visible controlled first touch |

**Live practice behavior**

- Show separate labels for the camera view and demonstration. Recording elapsed time and work/rest time are different displays.
- Show the recording indicator only after camera capture is confirmed. If recording stops, make that change explicit even if the child continues practising.
- In exercise mode, record active exercise periods as grouped clips. In full-session mode, programmed rests remain in the recording.
- **Pause** pauses practice and capture together. Resuming may create another recording part; show the gap truthfully.
- **Finish** ends the session, finalizes available local media, and opens review. Provide an immediate way to stop capture; do not trap the user in a confirmation flow while the camera continues.
- Pre-download the demo and spoken cues. Network loss should affect future synchronization, not interrupt a prepared local session.
- A front camera supports watching the same phone while being recorded. Rear camera mode assumes helper operation or audio-led practice. Validate distance and framing per drill.
- Design a landscape version of F09 with camera and demo side by side and a prominent timer. The current concept boards show portrait only.

**Exact parent/adult storage choices**

Cloud backup starts **off**. Enabling it opens the notice and allowance/retention choice before any video is uploaded. The concept board shows an example where the parent has already enabled it. Camera and microphone permission are separate; microphone starts off. Wi-Fi uploads are the default when backup is enabled.

The revised commercial proposal is Starter with local recording only, Family with 20 GB shared across up to four profiles, and Club with 5 GB per licensed player pooled under per-player safeguards. A complimentary pilot may receive an explicit temporary cloud allowance without becoming a paid subscription. Active cloud copies expire 30 days after successful upload; show each expiry, remaining capacity and separate local copies. Prices remain proposals, now specified in the end-to-end plan and adjustable workbook.

| User action | Required effect |
|---|---|
| Remove from device | Delete only that local copy; show whether a verified cloud copy remains |
| Remove cloud copy | Delete cloud media and thumbnails; suppress automatic re-upload of that asset; leave any local copy intact |
| Delete everywhere | Remove current authorized access and queue suppression/deletion for offline devices; respect the documented signed-link/cache window and already-exported-copy limits |
| Cloud allowance full | Pause uploads; keep local practice and playback available; show the next available action |
| Turn backup off | Stop/cancel queued uploads and explain how existing cloud copies are retained or deleted |
| Cloud retention expires | Delete the cloud copy and derived thumbnails; keep local copy if present; do not automatically re-upload the expired asset |
| Upload completes | Verify completion before labelling Backed up; deleting a local copy is still a separate owner choice |

**9. Light and dark appearance — design update, 6 September 2026**

**Confirmed direction:** the app must work with both the phone’s light and dark appearance. The user-approved recording concept establishes the navy-and-mint visual identity. This section supersedes the earlier fixed off-white/navy/lime palette. These are design requirements, not implemented functionality. Hex values below are proposed tokens, not exact samples extracted from the generated concept.

**Appearance behaviour**

- Provide Appearance choices: **Follow device (default)**, **Light**, and **Dark**. Follow device follows the operating system, including scheduled changes. On web, follow the browser/device preference by default with the same override choices.
- Apply the selected mode to every mobile and web screen, for players, parents, coaches, club administrators and platform administrators. Training is not forced dark, and administration is not forced light.
- Keep the override as a device-local preference, available offline and retained across app restarts. A restricted player may change appearance without entering protected adult settings or gaining extra permissions. Offer an accessible Appearance entry from ordinary non-recording settings; do not clutter active recording with settings.
- Reflect system changes while the app is open or when it resumes. Repaint presentation only: preserve navigation, forms, focus, timers, recording, playback, uploads and session state. A theme change must never reinitialize the camera or reset a drill.
- Match status/navigation bars, sheets, dialogs, menus, loading placeholders, keyboard appearance where supported, and launch/loading surfaces. Avoid a bright flash when opening in dark mode. Native permission and purchase dialogs remain controlled by the operating system.
