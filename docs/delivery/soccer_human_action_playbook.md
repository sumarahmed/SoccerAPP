6 September 2026 • Actions for the founder, specialists and accountable reviewers

Use this alongside the master plan and detailed backlog. Humans own the decisions and approvals below. Agents may prepare drafts, alternatives and evidence, but cannot impersonate a reviewer, invent a quote or obtain consent from a document alone. Roles are deliberately unassigned until real people accept them. One person can hold several operational roles where practical; independence and a genuine alternate remain necessary for the specific recovery/review cases that require them.

## 1. Assign people and authority

| Role | Accountable work | Must supply / approve | Backup requirement |
|---|---|---|---|
| Founder / product owner | Scope, budget, brand, packaging and business decisions | Dated decision register; actual funding and contact authority | Named decision substitute for absence where appropriate |
| Delivery owner | Board, dependencies, acceptance records and progress | Real assignees; resolved task contracts; current evidence | Another person able to identify blockers and suspend dispatch |
| Technical lead | Architecture, code review, security implementation and supported devices | Contract/migration decisions; acceptance of exact changes | Engineer/operator able to contain incidents |
| Qualified coach / content owner | Drill suitability, parameters, assessment and actual animation | Named approval for every released variant and asset | Appropriate specialist replacement; goalkeeper input where required |
| Animator / designer | Licensed motion assets, editable UI and accessibility | Source rights; paired mode components and states | Recoverable source assets and documented workflow |
| Privacy / security reviewers | Actual data flows, recovery, testing and release risks | Scoped findings, decisions, independent retest evidence | Escalation route; no fictional credentials or certification |
| Safeguarding owner | Sensitive reports and appropriate handling | Recipient rules; safe notification and alternative report route | A named alternative when the report concerns the primary handler |
| Operator / recovery custodian | Deployments, alerts, backups, restore and credentials | Coverage, tested runbooks, separate recovery/deletion authority | Genuine alternate with tested access |
| Research / club coordinator | Authorized recruitment and pilot observation | Consent/assent records stored separately, recruitment channels, findings | Consent-aware substitute |
| Billing / accounting adviser | Product configuration, taxes/terms, invoices, costs and refunds | Approved pricing and business treatment; dated financial model | Account access and support continuity |
| Release owner | Go/no-go and authorized publication | Exact artifact/config/content approval and rollout record | Named authorized substitute; avoid shared accounts |

## 2. Action cards in execution order

### H01 — Approve scope and resolve remaining assumptions

**Issues:** SP-001, SP-004, SP-075. **When:** P00.

**Read:** master plan section 2; baseline foundation, administration and business plans.

**Do:** confirm first market/language and how younger users receive assistance; preserve ages 5–18, both recording modes, standalone/club routes and the accepted hierarchy. Identify pilot representation and what evidence counts as success. List what is deliberately outside v1. Record owner/backup vacancies and the discovery spending limit. Separate authorized preparation from payments or external contact.

**Return:** signed/accepted decision record with each choice, reason, unresolved item, owner, review date and affected issue IDs. Do not replace an unresolved choice with an assumed approval. A material later change updates affected contracts and estimates.

### H02 — Establish brand ownership and export treatment

**Issues:** SP-076, SP-077, SP-080. **When:** P01; final assets before launch.

**Do:** select the app name through the appropriate brand/domain process; obtain original editable logo masters and commercial rights. Approve light/dark variants and a fixed navy export intro. Decide whether the proposed two-second logo and brief fade are correct. Approve how source audio, orientation and share/export permissions work. Use an explicitly labelled placeholder for experiments if the final brand is not settled.

**Return:** asset manifest with version, owner, rights, formats and intro storyboard. No live domain registration, supplier booking or publication is implied by this planning card.

### H03 — Commission and approve the curriculum

**Issues:** SP-005, SP-006, SP-125. **When:** samples in P01; complete released assets in P05.

**Do:** engage an appropriately qualified youth coach and required goalkeeper expertise. Review actual movement, age/ability eligibility, prerequisites, space, equipment, assistance, work/rest, cues and assessment. Approve three representative animation samples, then commission the complete per-variant asset inventory. Review foot/ball contact, captions and audio, not only the written brief.

**Return:** one approval record per exact variant/asset with qualifications scope, reviewer, date, corrections and rights. Twelve is the starter catalog, not the coach editor limit. Incomplete variants stay unpublished; an agent cannot certify physical suitability.

### H04 — Validate the complete experience in both themes

**Issues:** SP-007, SP-038, SP-062, SP-077, SP-130. **When:** P01 design; P08 real-device acceptance.

**Do:** review editable F01–F33 flows for youngest players, older beginners/teens, adult players, parents, coaches and club staff. Include permission denial, no-camera practice, quota, interruptions, safeguarding, MFA/recovery, account transitions and all Light/Dark states. Observe appropriately consented concept feedback using example data. Check whether a child several metres away can rely on numbers and cues. Confirm Appearance is reachable without opening protected adult controls.

**Return:** reviewed design versions, observed problems and corrections; no claim a concept test proves camera performance. Recheck implemented text scaling, focus, captions, contrast and outdoor usability before pilot.

### H05 — Approve data, privacy, recovery and safeguarding procedures

**Issues:** SP-008–SP-011, SP-037, SP-059–SP-063. **When:** P01; revalidate before real data.

**Do:** review every actor/resource/action and actual processor route. Approve retention by record class, sharing authority, guardianship disputes and age-18 transition. Decide acceptable session/link/offline residual windows. Approve a defensible lost-authenticator process with enrollment-only restoration and real second review for privileged recovery. Name safeguarding recipients and an alternative if a report concerns the primary handler. Commission specialist advice for the actual launch market and store audience.

**Return:** approved procedures and finding register with owner, due gate and exact affected flow. Do not ask families to send passwords, TOTP codes or seed secrets. Do not promise that all data stays in Australia solely because primary servers are there.

### H06 — Set up accounts, board, repository and bounded agency

**Issues:** SP-002, SP-003, SP-012, SP-013, SP-146. **When:** P02 after the relevant implementation scope is commissioned.

**Do:** select actual organization-owned accounts and enable suitable account protections. Import tasks through the guide, confirm mappings, assign human owners/reviewers and limit agent access. Choose exact repositories/environments and spend/time ceilings. Prove branch/check/review restrictions with one harmless failing change and one bounded run. Record what the chosen integration cannot enforce automatically.

**Return:** access inventory and successful task/failed-check evidence. Account setup does not imply permission to invite people, incur charges or publish. Once authorized, routine work inside that scope proceeds without repeated confirmations.

### H07 — Set device targets and accept feasibility

**Issues:** SP-126, SP-014–SP-018, SP-039, SP-051–SP-052, SP-065, SP-081. **When:** P03.

**Do:** define the named devices and measurable tolerances before tests. Supply synthetic/consenting-adult data and no-charge sandboxes. Review camera start/finalize, timers, long capture, interrupts, export, local migrations, MFA, player credentials, access denial, upload and billing results. Compare proposed fallback and device support options. Re-estimate engineering, content, security and operations from this evidence.

**Return:** go/revise/stop decision with accepted limits, exact tested artifacts, quoted versus estimated costs and a funded next scope. Do not fund the full product based solely on package documentation or a simulator recording.

### H08 — Review implementation contracts and acceptance evidence

**Issues:** SP-019–SP-027, SP-040–SP-045, SP-053, SP-055, SP-066–SP-070. **When:** P04–P08.

**Do:** review bounded changes in small batches. Check database migrations, ownership, adult/player sessions, publication and grants. Have the coach approve actual published assets and assessment wording. Review timer/demo/recording interactions, original/export timelines, audio and theme switching. Verify positive functionality and negative access cases. Reject missing or stale evidence; ask for the specific failing case, not a broad rewrite.

**Return:** recorded review decision tied to commit/build/config or content/design version. Review does not mean the issue is deployed, live or commercially accepted. Work packages close only when children and their original integration criteria are satisfied.

### H09 — Independently accept security and pilot operation

**Issues:** SP-026, SP-028, SP-071, SP-128, SP-130, SP-131. **When:** P08.

**Do:** arrange an independent scope-based assessment and retest of blockers. Test real support/safeguarding recipient routing and backup operator access. Rehearse restoration and compromised credentials with synthetic records; confirm deletion/revocation is replayed. Confirm devices/content are supported, all required assets are approved and pilot permissions/metrics are ready.

**Return:** signed pilot acceptance by the actual founder, technical lead and coach, with relevant specialist evidence. No real children's practice data is used to discover basic camera or identity feasibility failures.

### H10 — Run controlled research and correct problems

**Issues:** SP-029, SP-046, SP-057, SP-137–SP-140. **When:** P09.

**Do:** recruit through authorized channels; obtain separate research and product/media choices. Include standalone and club-sponsored cases across intended ages, devices and assistance levels. Observe installation/setup, explanation, training, recording/export, no-camera completion, coach plan edits, sharing, offboarding and recovery. Record denominators and support time. Honour withdrawal and retain minimal private research information outside ordinary issues.

**Return:** de-identified findings, defects, corrected/retested evidence, adult value/price response and a revised release scope. Agents may analyse the permitted dataset but cannot recruit or consent participants by generating a form.

### H11 — Approve actual subscriptions and operating economics

**Issues:** SP-048, SP-049, SP-054, SP-057, SP-072, SP-073, SP-147, SP-148. **When:** contracts P01; actual approval P10.

**Do:** review monthly/annual family and club value, tax presentation, seat minimum, staff inclusion, quotas, retention, cancellation/refund paths and purchase-source overlap. Reconcile forecasts with observed media/support/validation/export/recovery usage and current supplier costs. Include founder capacity, independent reviews, software accounts, recurring support and cash runway. Separate test product configuration from real prices/charges.

**Return:** approved catalog and dated financial model; named billing authority and support instructions. The earlier model remains a baseline until this work updates it. Payment never changes guardianship or video-viewing rights.

### H12 — Prepare business and release ownership

**Issues:** SP-032, SP-033, SP-056, SP-134–SP-136. **When:** P10.

**Do:** complete appropriate entity, IP, contractual, accounting, tax, insurance and privacy work with specialists. Ensure actual store forms and notices match the implementation. Approve support response promises that the team can meet, domain/email ownership and recovery credentials. Review staged rollout, configuration, backups and old-client compatibility. Put actual public-facing terms/help in place before sales.

**Return:** approved business/release package, store assets and support/incident coverage. This document identifies required business work, not legal advice or a claim of compliance.

### H13 — Approve and publish the exact release

**Issues:** SP-034, SP-074, SP-141, SP-150. **When:** P10.

**Do:** check all release gates against the current signed artifact, config, content and evidence manifest. Confirm no unresolved blocker within the advertised scope. Record authorized audience, rollout size/timing and decision expiry. Publish through the authorized owner; track submitted, approved and live separately. Verify the limited live rollout and response ownership before expansion.

**Return:** attributable release decision and actual availability/smoke evidence. An agent can prepare every reviewable artifact but cannot substitute a generated approval for the designated release owner.

### H14 — Operate and decide subsequent investment

**Issues:** SP-035, SP-036, SP-058, SP-142–SP-145. **When:** P11 and each dated operating cycle.

**Do:** review incidents, reliability, actual spend, renewals, support, dependencies, access, restoration, retention, content and adulthood transfers. Configure recurring issues only within an agreed cadence. Assess demand for Coach Pro/add-ons/additional markets before spending. Keep vendor portability and service-exit procedures current.

**Return:** dated maintenance decisions, accepted changes, updated budgets and explicit expansion proposals. Do not promise indefinite autonomous operation without funded people and exception handling.

## 3. Weekly review agenda

1. Inspect due decisions, high-impact failures and the oldest blocker; name the person and next evidence needed.
2. Review only changes with complete evidence. Resolve reviewer capacity before starting more work.
3. Check cost/time limits, supplier lead time, content production and required device availability.
4. Choose dependency-ready leaf tasks for the next cycle; record exact scope and reuse established authorization.
5. Update gate evidence and material changes. Record any scope change and its product, security, content and commercial consequences.

The meeting produces decisions and issue changes, not another competing tracker. Keep private consent, recovery and safeguarding records in their approved restricted system; issues contain only redacted reference IDs and required action.

## 4. Human acceptance record template

| Field | Required content |
|---|---|
| Decision / source issue | Stable issue ID and exact outcome under review |
| Accountable person | Actual name and role; do not use a placeholder as approval |
| Evidence | Exact version/build/config/content plus test/review references |
| Decision | Accept, return for changes, reject, or accept an explicitly permitted scoped exception |
| Limits | Unsupported cases, remaining residual windows and affected release scope |
| Authorization | Approved action, environment, audience and spending boundary where applicable |
| Date / expiry | Actual decision time and event/date that requires renewed review |
| Next owner / action | Concrete follow-up for every unresolved item |

Approval applies only to the recorded scope/version. A material change invalidates the affected acceptance until re-reviewed. Routine authorized reversible work does not need a new permission request on every step.
