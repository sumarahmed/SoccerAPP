# SoccerTrainingApp — Competitive Market Gap Analysis

Research edition: 6 September 2026

## Summary

The planned product already covers many expected category capabilities: training content, timers, recording, offline use, parent/club administration, progress history, subscriptions and coach workflows. Those are necessary, but not individually distinctive.

The strongest product position to validate is:

> **Age-appropriate practice between club sessions, with a clear next activity, reliable optional recording, useful feedback and family-controlled privacy.**

The largest gaps versus established training products are curriculum depth, guidance on what to practise next, meaningful progress evidence, repeat-use mechanics and a fast coach-feedback loop.

## Relevant competitor set

The original review used direct training/content competitors such as Techne Futbol, Train Effective, Dribbleup, Coerver and MyPersonalFootballCoach, plus adjacent club platforms such as 360Player and TeamSnap. Public vendor claims were treated as advertised capability, not hands-on verification.

## Where our current requirements are strong

- Direct-family and club-managed routes in one product.
- Ages 5–18 with guardian authority and adult transition.
- Optional recording instead of forcing video for participation.
- Exercise-clip and full-session recording modes.
- Local-first/offline training.
- Private cloud backup rather than public social sharing.
- Explicit scoped media grants and revocation.
- Logo-first export and branded family/club output.
- Light/Dark support and outdoor-use requirements.
- Adult MFA, restricted player sessions and defined recovery.
- Coach age-plan editing, assignment and scoped progress.
- Stronger privacy/safeguarding design than a generic social/community model.

## Immediate product gaps to close

### 1. Next-practice guidance

A player should not finish a drill and face an unstructured catalog. The first version should recommend the next approved activity using curriculum prerequisites, age/ability and completed work — without pretending to provide autonomous expert diagnosis.

### 2. Visible progression pathways

Programs and positions need an understandable progression structure. Players and parents should be able to see what is being learned, what prerequisites exist and what comes next.

### 3. Meaningful private progress evidence

Completion history, consistency and coach-approved milestones should be visible. Avoid speculative technique scores or public rankings until the measurement method is genuinely validated.

### 4. Closed coach feedback loop

A guardian/adult should be able to deliberately submit an authorized attempt to an assigned coach. The coach needs a bounded queue, structured improvement cue and linked next drill. Withdrawal/expiry must revoke new access. Do not create unrestricted private adult–child messaging.

### 5. Healthy weekly goals

Use optional weekly practice goals, private milestones and adult-configured reminders. Do not make recording mandatory, punish missed weeks, erase earned progress or optimize for screen time.

## Competitive features that remain wishlist rather than v1

- Automated camera/pose/ball technique scoring.
- Live rotatable 3D demonstration rendering.
- Public leaderboards and social feeds.
- Talent prediction/scouting claims.
- Coach marketplace or platform-provided mentoring.
- Unrestricted direct messaging.
- Hardware-dependent smart-ball tracking.
- Advanced club integrations/imports before core workflows are validated.

## Commercial implication

Club administration alone competes with mature low-cost platforms. The product needs to earn a place through training value between team sessions, family-controlled video, progression and coach feedback — not by trying to replace every club-management function in the first release.

Family and club prices in earlier planning are assumptions, not approved live offers. Pricing should be re-tested after actual media/storage/support usage and pilot willingness-to-pay data are available.

## Recommendation

Reconcile the five immediate priorities into the existing delivery backlog before implementation dispatch. Preserve the original feasibility, recording, export, offline, security, consent, deletion, subscription and support work; the market priorities supplement rather than replace that baseline.
